# Glossary — every term and acronym in this notebook, defined once

> **This page has a different shape from the rest of the notebook.** Other pages derive one mechanism in depth. This page is a lookup table: it exists so that every *other* page can be read without stopping. It is also a study tool — each entry states what the thing **is**, what problem it **solves**, and which page **derives** it.

---

## How to use this page

Chip design has perhaps a thousand acronyms in active daily use, and almost none of them are optional. A sentence like "the SS-corner setup path failed after CTS because OCV derate ate the CPPR credit" is not jargon for its own sake — every one of those words names a distinct physical or algorithmic object, and removing any of them makes the sentence untrue. The cost of that density is that a newcomer cannot read a single real design review email. This page removes that cost.

Three conventions govern the entries below. First, **a bolded term in the first column is defined here and only here** — the rest of the notebook may bold a term at first use, but this page is the canonical definition. Second, **the definition column always says what problem the thing solves**, never just what the letters stand for; "PVT = process, voltage, temperature" teaches nothing, whereas "the three axes along which a fabricated transistor differs from the nominal one you simulated, so timing must be closed at several combinations of them" teaches the reason the concept exists. Third, **the third column is a link to the page that derives the concept** — not a page that mentions it, the page that builds it from a baseline, breaks the baseline, and repairs it. An em dash (—) means no single page owns the term; it is either shared vocabulary or background you already have.

Read this page in one of three ways. As a **dictionary**: search for the acronym, read one row, go back to what you were reading. As a **map**: skim a letter section and follow every link that surprises you — the third column is effectively an index of the notebook organized by vocabulary instead of by flow stage. As a **self-test**: cover the definition column and try to state, for each term, what breaks if the thing does not exist. If you can do that for 300 of these, you can hold a design review.

The entries are grouped into alphabetical sections. Numerals and standards numbered like `1149.1` are in the `0-9` section. Where one acronym has two unrelated meanings (SDC, DFA, RAS, SC, MPW, IR, and several others), each meaning gets its own row — collisions like these are a real and frequent source of confusion in cross-discipline meetings, so they are called out rather than hidden.

## How to read a chip-design sentence

The single most useful thing to learn early is that this vocabulary is **compositional**. A dense sentence is not one opaque idiom; it is five or six independent nouns, each of which you can look up, chained by ordinary causality. Once you see the seams, the sentence decodes itself. Here are three sentences taken from the register of real design reviews, decoded fragment by fragment.

### Sentence 1 — a timing-closure sentence

> *"We missed setup at the SS corner after CTS because the OCV derate on a deep clock path ate the CPPR credit."*

| Fragment | What it actually says | Where it is derived |
|---|---|---|
| *missed setup* | A setup check failed: data arrived at a capture flip-flop's D input **later** than one clock period minus the flop's setup requirement. Slack is negative, so this path would sample the wrong value on real silicon. | [STA](06_Signoff/01_STA.md) |
| *at the SS corner* | At the slow-slow process corner — a library characterized with both NMOS and PMOS transistors at the slow end of the fabrication distribution, usually paired with low supply voltage. It is the corner where gate delays are largest, so it is where max-delay (setup) checks bite. | [Standard-Cell Libraries and Characterization](04_Synthesis/03_Standard_Cell_Libraries_and_Characterization.md) |
| *after CTS* | After clock tree synthesis, i.e. after the ideal zero-delay clock assumed during synthesis was replaced by a real buffer tree with real insertion delay, real skew, and real variation exposure. Timing that passed pre-CTS routinely fails post-CTS; that is the expected order of events, not a surprise. | [Clock Tree Synthesis](05_Backend_Physical_Design/05_Clock_Tree_Synthesis.md) |
| *the OCV derate* | On-chip variation derating: multiplying launch-path delays up and capture-path delays down by a percentage, to model the fact that two identical cells on the same die do not have identical delay. It is deliberate pessimism bought because a single corner cannot express within-die spread. | [STA](06_Signoff/01_STA.md) |
| *on a deep clock path* | The launch and capture flops sit many buffer stages down the clock tree. Derate is multiplicative, so a large absolute clock delay turns a 5% derate into a large absolute number of picoseconds. Depth is the amplifier. | [Clock Tree Synthesis](05_Backend_Physical_Design/05_Clock_Tree_Synthesis.md) |
| *ate the CPPR credit* | Clock-path-pessimism removal gives back the derate that was applied to the **common** portion of the launch and capture clock paths, because one physical buffer cannot be simultaneously fast and slow. If the two paths diverge early, the common portion is short, the credit is small, and the pessimism survives to the slack number. | [STA](06_Signoff/01_STA.md) |

**What the sentence means end to end:** the path is real-silicon-safe, but the *analysis* says it fails, because the clock tree is deep and the launch/capture branches split near the root, so the tool applied variation pessimism to a long stretch of clock that it could not then refund. **What you do about it:** rebalance the tree so the two flops share more clock path, reduce tree depth, apply useful skew, or move from flat OCV to POCV/LVF so the pessimism is statistical rather than a flat multiplier. Note that four of the six fragments were about the *clock*, not the logic — that is the usual shape of a post-CTS setup failure.

### Sentence 2 — a verification sentence

> *"Regression was green in RTL but GLS failed: an X from a non-reset flop propagated out the AXI B channel, and the SDF hold check on scan_enable fired at the FF corner."*

| Fragment | What it actually says | Where it is derived |
|---|---|---|
| *regression was green* | The full automated suite of directed and constrained-random tests ran overnight and all passed. Green means "no test failed", which is a much weaker statement than "the design is correct" — coverage, not pass rate, is the real metric. | [Verification Planning and Coverage Closure](03_Frontend_RTL_and_Verification/11_Verification_Planning_and_Coverage_Closure.md) |
| *in RTL* | In register-transfer-level simulation, where every gate is a behavioral expression evaluated in zero time and the only timing is the clock edge. Fast, but it hides everything structural. | [RTL Design Methodology](03_Frontend_RTL_and_Verification/01_RTL_Design_Methodology.md) |
| *but GLS failed* | Gate-level simulation — the same tests run on the synthesized netlist of real library cells. It is slow and it is run precisely because it catches the class of bug RTL simulation cannot see: reset coverage, X semantics, DFT logic, and real cell delays. | [Gate-Level Sim and Emulation](03_Frontend_RTL_and_Verification/13_Gate_Level_Sim_and_Emulation.md) |
| *an X* | An unknown value in four-state simulation. RTL `if` statements resolve an X optimistically (they pick a branch), while a gate-level mux propagates it — so a design that "works" in RTL can be genuinely uninitialized in silicon. | [Data Types and Basics](03_Frontend_RTL_and_Verification/02_Data_Types_and_Basics.md) |
| *from a non-reset flop* | A flip-flop with no reset pin. This is a legitimate area/power optimization for datapath registers, but it means the flop powers up unknown and must be *functionally* initialized before its value is ever used. Someone assumed it was reset. | [RTL Design Patterns](03_Frontend_RTL_and_Verification/14_RTL_Design_Patterns.md) |
| *out the AXI B channel* | The write-response channel of an AXI interface — one of five independent channels, the one that reports whether a write completed. An X escaping onto a protocol boundary means the bug is now everyone's bug, not just this block's. | [AHB, AXI, APB](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/03_Transaction_Protocols/01_AHB_AXI_APB.md) |
| *the SDF hold check* | A hold check evaluated with back-annotated Standard Delay Format cell and net delays, i.e. GLS with real timing. Hold failures depend on *minimum* delay, which is why they show up at the fast corner and are invisible in unit-delay simulation. | [STA](06_Signoff/01_STA.md) |
| *on scan_enable* | The global signal that switches every scan flip-flop between functional and shift mode. It fans out to every flop in the design, so it is one of the highest-fanout, latest-built, most hold-critical nets on the chip. | [DFT and ATPG](06_Signoff/02_DFT_and_ATPG.md) |
| *at the FF corner* | Fast-fast: fast NMOS, fast PMOS, typically high voltage. Minimum delays are smallest here, so this is where hold violations appear. Setup fails at SS, hold fails at FF — that asymmetry is the whole reason more than one corner is signed off. | [STA](06_Signoff/01_STA.md) |

**What the sentence means end to end:** two independent bugs surfaced in the same run — a functional initialization gap that only a four-state gate-level model can expose, and a physical min-delay problem on test infrastructure that only back-annotated timing can expose. Neither is an RTL bug in the sense of "the logic is wrong"; both are hand-off bugs between stages, which is the characteristic failure mode this notebook is organized to prevent.

### Sentence 3 — an AI-serving sentence

> *"Decode is memory-bound at batch 1 — every token drags the whole KV cache across HBM, so we sit at 4% of peak BF16 throughput until continuous batching and paged attention raise arithmetic intensity; that fixes TPOT, not TTFT."*

| Fragment | What it actually says | Where it is derived |
|---|---|---|
| *decode* | The autoregressive phase of transformer inference, one token per step, each step depending on the previous one. Structurally it is a sequence of matrix-vector products, which is the worst possible shape for a machine built for matrix-matrix products. | [End-to-End GPU AI Inference and Serving](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/05_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/02_End_to_End_GPU_AI_Inference_and_Serving.md) |
| *is memory-bound* | The kernel's runtime is set by bytes moved, not by arithmetic performed — it sits on the sloped part of the roofline. Adding FLOP/s to the machine changes nothing; adding bandwidth or reducing bytes does. | [GPU Workloads, Performance and DSE](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/00_Design_Methodology/01_GPU_Workloads_Performance_and_DSE.md) |
| *at batch 1* | One request in flight. Every weight byte read from memory is used for exactly one multiply-accumulate, so arithmetic intensity is about 2 FLOP per byte — far below the machine balance point of a few hundred. | [AI Workload and Operator Mapping](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/05_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/01_AI_Workload_and_Operator_Mapping.md) |
| *the whole KV cache* | The stored key and value tensors for every previous token of every active sequence. It grows linearly with context length and batch size, and unlike weights it cannot be shared across requests. | [GPU Serving Engine, Scheduler and KV Implementation Blueprint](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/05_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/05_GPU_Serving_Engine_Scheduler_and_KV_Implementation_Blueprint.md) |
| *across HBM* | High-bandwidth memory: stacked DRAM dies connected by through-silicon vias over a very wide, relatively slow interface. It supplies terabytes per second, which is enormous and still not enough for this access pattern. | [HBM and Advanced Memory Systems](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/02_Memory_System/02_HBM_and_Advanced_Memory_Systems.md) |
| *4% of peak BF16* | Achieved throughput divided by the datasheet number, where BF16 is the 16-bit brain-float format with FP32's exponent range and 8 fraction bits. A single-digit percentage is normal and expected for decode — quoting peak here is the classic performance-analysis error. | [Floating Point](00_Fundamentals/04_Floating_Point.md) |
| *continuous batching* | Admitting and retiring requests at token granularity instead of running fixed batches to completion, so the GPU always has many sequences in flight. It converts many matrix-vector products into one matrix-matrix product, which is the only structural fix for the intensity problem. | [GPU Serving Engine, Scheduler and KV Implementation Blueprint](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/05_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/05_GPU_Serving_Engine_Scheduler_and_KV_Implementation_Blueprint.md) |
| *paged attention* | Storing the KV cache in fixed-size non-contiguous blocks with an indirection table, exactly like virtual memory pages, so that batching is not blocked by the need for one huge contiguous allocation per sequence. | [Transformer and Attention Engine Microarchitecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/03_NPU_Architecture/01_Compute_Dataflows/03_Transformer_and_Attention_Engine_Microarchitecture.md) |
| *arithmetic intensity* | FLOP performed per byte moved from memory. It is the x-axis of the roofline model and the single number that decides whether a kernel is compute- or memory-limited. | [GPU Workloads, Performance and DSE](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/00_Design_Methodology/01_GPU_Workloads_Performance_and_DSE.md) |
| *fixes TPOT, not TTFT* | Time per output token improves because the machine is better utilized; time to first token does not, because the prefill phase was already compute-bound and batching adds queueing delay to it. Two service-level objectives that pull in opposite directions. | [End-to-End GPU AI Inference and Serving](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/05_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/02_End_to_End_GPU_AI_Inference_and_Serving.md) |

**The general lesson.** In all three sentences the structure is identical: *a symptom*, *the operating condition that exposes it*, *the mechanism that causes it*, and *the mechanism that was supposed to prevent it but did not apply*. If you can parse a dense sentence into those four slots, you can usually work out what the speaker will propose next. That skill, not memorization, is what this glossary is for.

| **1T1C cell** | The DRAM storage cell: one access transistor plus one capacitor. | **8T / 10T SRAM cell** | SRAM bitcells with a separate read port added to the 6T core. | **IEEE 1076** | The VHDL language standard. | — |

It solves board-level test after surface-mount packaging made physical probing impossible, and is then reused for debug access. | [DFT and ATPG](06_Signoff/02_DFT_and_ATPG.md) |
| **IEEE 1364** | The Verilog language standard, superseded for design purposes by IEEE 1800. | — |

It exists because power intent cannot be expressed in RTL, yet every downstream tool needs it. | [UPF and CPF Power Intent](02_Power_and_Low_Power/05_UPF_and_CPF_Power_Intent.md) |
| **ISO 26262** | The automotive functional-safety standard. It defines ASIL levels, the required metrics (SPFM, LFM, PMHF), and the development process evidence needed to argue a chip is safe enough for a given hazard. | [Functional Safety and Reliability Engineering](08_Cross_Cutting_Engineering/02_Functional_Safety_and_Reliability_Engineering.md) |

## A

| Term / acronym | Definition | Where it is derived |
|---|---|---|
| **AER (advanced error reporting)** | The PCIe capability that classifies an error as correctable, uncorrectable non-fatal, or fatal and records which packet caused it. Without it a link error is visible only as a hang or a bad completion. | [PCI Express Protocol Deep Dive](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/05_IO_and_Chiplets/04_PCIe_Protocol_Deep_Dive.md) |
| **ASPM (active state power management)** | PCIe link power states entered automatically when the link is idle. It saves real power and adds exit latency, which is why latency-sensitive accelerators often disable it. | [PCI Express Protocol Deep Dive](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/05_IO_and_Chiplets/04_PCIe_Protocol_Deep_Dive.md) |
| **ABB (adaptive body bias)** | Dynamically adjusting transistor body/well voltage to trade threshold voltage against leakage, per die or per temperature. It compensates process spread after fabrication rather than designing for the worst case. | [Power Reduction Techniques](02_Power_and_Low_Power/04_Power_Reduction_Techniques.md) |
| **ABFT (algorithm-based fault tolerance)** | Adding checksum rows and columns to a matrix computation so that errors in the result can be detected and sometimes located, without duplicating the hardware. It buys error coverage for a few percent overhead where full redundancy would cost 100%. | [Host Interface, Memory Visibility and Scheduling](01_Architecture_and_PPA/03_NPU_Architecture/03_System_Integration/01_Host_Interface_Memory_Visibility_and_Scheduling.md) |
| **ABI (application binary interface)** | The calling convention, register usage, and data layout contract between compiled code modules. Hardware cares because it fixes which registers must be preserved and therefore what the spill/fill traffic looks like. | [RISC-V ISA](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/01_Core_Foundations/02_RISC_V_ISA.md) |
| **ACE (AXI Coherency Extensions)** | Arm's extension of AXI with snoop channels and cache-line state signaling, so that caches attached to the same interconnect stay coherent. It solves the problem that plain AXI has no way to invalidate someone else's copy. | [ACE and CHI](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/06_Coherence_and_Consistency/03_ACE_and_CHI.md) |
| **ACE-Lite** | The one-way subset of ACE for masters that must see coherent data but hold no coherent cache of their own, such as a DMA engine or GPU. It gets IO coherence without the cost of implementing snoop response logic. | [QoS, Ordering and IO Coherence](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/05_IO_and_Chiplets/01_QoS_Ordering_and_IO_Coherence.md) |
| **Activity factor ($\alpha$)** | The average number of 0→1 transitions a node makes per clock cycle. It is the term in $P = \alpha C V^2 f$ that design decisions actually control, which is why clock gating and data gating are the first power levers. | [Power Fundamentals](02_Power_and_Low_Power/01_Power_Fundamentals.md) |
| **Adaptive voltage scaling (AVS)** | Closing a feedback loop from on-chip delay monitors back to the regulator, so the supply sits just above what this die at this temperature actually needs. It recovers the margin that fixed-voltage DVFS must leave for worst-case silicon. | [Power Reduction Techniques](02_Power_and_Low_Power/04_Power_Reduction_Techniques.md) |
| **AES (Advanced Encryption Standard)** | The standard block cipher. In hardware it appears as an accelerator whose real design problems are throughput per area, key handling, and resistance to power analysis rather than the cipher itself. | [Hardware Security Architecture](08_Cross_Cutting_Engineering/01_Hardware_Security_Architecture.md) |
| **Aging** | The cumulative slowing of transistors over service life from BTI, HCI, and TDDB. It is why signoff uses an end-of-life derated library rather than fresh-silicon timing, and why guardband is spent on time as well as on variation. | [Signal Integrity and Reliability](05_Backend_Physical_Design/02_Signal_Integrity_Reliability.md) |
| **AGU (address generation unit)** | The small adder that computes an effective memory address from base, index, scale, and displacement. It exists as a separate unit because address arithmetic must complete a cycle or more before the data access, so it sits on the critical loop of load latency. | [Load-Store Unit and Memory Ordering](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/03_Out_of_Order_Backend/02_Load_Store_Unit_and_Memory_Ordering.md) |
| **AHB (Advanced High-performance Bus)** | An AMBA bus with a single shared address phase and data phase, pipelined but not split. It is the right answer for moderate-bandwidth on-chip traffic where AXI's five channels and outstanding-transaction bookkeeping would be overkill. | [AHB, AXI, APB](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/03_Transaction_Protocols/01_AHB_AXI_APB.md) |
| **ALD (atomic layer deposition)** | Depositing material one atomic monolayer at a time by alternating self-limiting surface reactions. It is how gate dielectrics and liners thin enough to be measured in atoms are built with usable uniformity. | [Fabrication Process](07_Manufacturing_and_Bringup/01_Fabrication_Process.md) |
| **ALU (arithmetic logic unit)** | The integer execution unit performing add, subtract, logical, shift, and compare operations. Its latency defines the back-to-back dependent-instruction rate, which is why single-cycle ALU bypass is one of the hardest paths in a fast core. | [CPU Architecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/01_Core_Foundations/01_CPU_Architecture.md) |
| **AMAT (average memory access time)** | Hit time plus miss rate times miss penalty, applied recursively down the hierarchy. It is the simplest model that shows why a small fast cache in front of a large slow one beats either alone. | [Cache Microarchitecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/04_Cache_Hierarchy/01_Cache_Microarchitecture.md) |
| **AMBA** | Arm's family of on-chip interconnect specifications (APB, AHB, AXI, ACE, CHI). Its value is that IP from different vendors composes without glue logic. | [AHB, AXI, APB](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/03_Transaction_Protocols/01_AHB_AXI_APB.md) |
| **Amdahl's law** | Speedup is bounded by $1/((1-p) + p/s)$ where $p$ is the parallelizable fraction. It is the reason accelerators are judged on the whole application rather than the kernel, and the reason a 100× kernel speedup routinely yields 1.3× end to end. | [CPU Architecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/01_Core_Foundations/01_CPU_Architecture.md) |
| **AMO (atomic memory operation)** | A read-modify-write that the memory system guarantees to perform indivisibly, such as fetch-and-add or compare-and-swap. It solves synchronization without locking the entire bus, and its placement (in the core or at the shared cache) is a major coherence design decision. | [Memory Consistency and Atomics](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/06_Coherence_and_Consistency/02_Memory_Consistency_and_Atomics.md) |
| **AMX (Advanced Matrix Extensions)** | Intel's tile-register matrix-multiply extension, adding a small 2D register file and a TMUL unit. It brings systolic-style dense matrix throughput into a CPU core without a separate accelerator and its coherence problems. | [AI Operators on CPU Microarchitecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/09_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/02_AI_Operators_on_CPU_Microarchitecture.md) |
| **Annotation (back-annotation)** | Replacing estimated delays or parasitics in an analysis with values extracted from the actual layout, via SDF or SPEF. It is the step that turns an optimistic pre-layout number into a signoff-grade one. | [Routing and Parasitic Extraction](05_Backend_Physical_Design/06_Routing_and_Parasitic_Extraction.md) |
| **Antenna effect** | Charge collected by a long metal segment during plasma etch discharging through, and damaging, a thin gate oxide before the protecting diode is connected. The fix is antenna diodes or metal jumpers, and the rule is checked as a DRC. | [Physical Verification DRC/LVS](06_Signoff/03_Physical_Verification_DRC_LVS.md) |
| **AOCV (advanced on-chip variation)** | Derating that varies with path depth and physical span rather than a single flat percentage, on the argument that random variation averages out along a long path. It removes pessimism that flat OCV cannot distinguish. | [STA](06_Signoff/01_STA.md) |
| **AOI / OAI gate** | And-Or-Invert and Or-And-Invert compound cells. They implement two levels of logic in one CMOS stack, which is faster and smaller than the equivalent discrete gates because CMOS is naturally inverting. | [Logic Building Blocks](00_Fundamentals/02_Logic_Building_Blocks.md) |
| **AON (always-on)** | Logic and its supply that must remain powered while other domains are shut down — retention control, isolation enables, wake logic. It is the part of a power-gated design that cannot itself be power gated. | [Low-Power Architecture and Domain Partitioning](02_Power_and_Low_Power/03_Low_Power_Architecture_and_Domain_Partitioning.md) |
| **APB (Advanced Peripheral Bus)** | A simple non-pipelined AMBA bus for low-bandwidth control registers. It exists so that a hundred slow peripherals do not each burden the high-performance interconnect with address decode and arbitration. | [AHB, AXI, APB](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/03_Transaction_Protocols/01_AHB_AXI_APB.md) |
| **Arbitration** | Choosing which of several competing requesters gets a shared resource this cycle. The policy — round-robin, fixed priority, weighted, age-based — is where fairness, starvation, and worst-case latency bounds are actually decided. | [Routing, Flow Control and Deadlock](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/04_On_Chip_Networks/02_Routing_Flow_Control_and_Deadlock.md) |
| **Arithmetic intensity** | FLOP performed per byte transferred from memory. It places a kernel on the roofline and therefore decides whether more compute or more bandwidth is the useful investment. | [GPU Workloads, Performance and DSE](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/00_Design_Methodology/01_GPU_Workloads_Performance_and_DSE.md) |

It is the SLO that determines perceived generation speed, and it is bandwidth-limited rather than compute-limited. | [End-to-End GPU AI Inference and Serving](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/05_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/02_End_to_End_GPU_AI_Inference_and_Serving.md) |
| **Track** | A legal routing centerline on a metal layer. Track count per row and per gcell is the supply side of the congestion equation, and cell pin access must land on tracks to be routable. | [Routing and Parasitic Extraction](05_Backend_Physical_Design/06_Routing_and_Parasitic_Extraction.md) |
| **Transition fault** | See *TDF*. | [DFT and ATPG](06_Signoff/02_DFT_and_ATPG.md) |
| **tRCD / tRP / tRAS / tREFI / tFAW** | DRAM timing parameters: RAS-to-CAS delay, precharge time, minimum row active time, refresh interval, and the four-activate window. Together they are why DRAM bandwidth depends on access pattern rather than being a single number. | [DDR Controller](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/02_Shared_Memory/01_DDR_Controller.md) |
| **TRR (target row refresh)** | A DRAM mitigation that refreshes rows adjacent to one being hammered. It is the first-generation RowHammer defense, later supplemented by refresh-management commands. | [DDR Controller](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/02_Shared_Memory/01_DDR_Controller.md) |
| **TrustZone** | Arm's two-world (secure and non-secure) partitioning enforced across the core, interconnect, and peripherals. It exists so isolation is enforced by hardware attributes on every transaction rather than by software convention. | [Hardware Security Architecture](08_Cross_Cutting_Engineering/01_Hardware_Security_Architecture.md) |
| **TSO (total store order)** | The x86-style memory model where the only permitted reordering is a load passing an older store to a different address. It is strong enough that most naive concurrent code works and weak enough to permit a store buffer. | [Memory Consistency and Atomics](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/06_Coherence_and_Consistency/02_Memory_Consistency_and_Atomics.md) |
| **TSV (through-silicon via)** | A vertical conductor passing through a thinned die. It is what makes HBM stacks and 3D integration electrically possible, and it consumes area and imposes keepout rules on the die it passes through. | [IC Packaging](07_Manufacturing_and_Bringup/02_IC_Packaging.md) |
| **TT corner** | Typical-typical: nominal devices, nominal voltage. It is used for power estimation and sanity checks, and it is never sufficient for timing signoff on its own. | [STA](06_Signoff/01_STA.md) |
| **TTFT (time to first token)** | Latency from request arrival to the first generated token, dominated by prefill and queueing. It is the SLO that determines perceived responsiveness and it trades against throughput. | [End-to-End GPU AI Inference and Serving](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/05_AI_Workloads_and_Serving/02_End_to_End_GPU_AI_Inference_and_Serving.md) |
| **Turn model** | A deadlock-avoidance routing scheme that forbids a specific subset of turns to break all cycles in the channel dependency graph. It buys deadlock freedom without virtual channels, at the cost of path diversity. | [Routing, Flow Control and Deadlock](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/04_On_Chip_Networks/02_Routing_Flow_Control_and_Deadlock.md) |

## V

| Term / acronym | Definition | Where it is derived |
|---|---|---|
| **Valid/ready handshake** | A transfer occurs on a clock edge where both `valid` and `ready` are high, with `valid` never waiting on `ready`. Its value is that it composes: any two modules obeying it can be connected without a rate-matching negotiation. | [Flow Control and FIFOs](03_Frontend_RTL_and_Verification/15_Flow_Control_and_FIFOs.md) |
| **VC (virtual channel)** | Several independent buffer queues sharing one physical link. They break head-of-line blocking and provide the separate dependency classes needed for protocol-level deadlock freedom, at the cost of buffers and arbitration. | [Routing, Flow Control and Deadlock](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/04_On_Chip_Networks/02_Routing_Flow_Control_and_Deadlock.md) |
| **VCD (value change dump)** | A plain-text waveform format recording every signal transition. It is universal and enormous, which is why compressed proprietary formats exist for large dumps. | [Block Activity and Power](02_Power_and_Low_Power/02_Block_Activity_and_Power.md) |
| **Vdd / Vss** | The positive supply and ground rails. Naming them explicitly matters because everything from noise margin through dynamic power to IR drop is expressed relative to them. | [CMOS Fundamentals](00_Fundamentals/01_CMOS_Fundamentals.md) |
| **Vector length (VLEN / LMUL)** | In a scalable vector ISA, the hardware register width and the software-selected register grouping. They exist so that one binary runs on implementations with different vector widths, which fixed-width SIMD cannot do. | [SMT, SIMD and Vector Execution](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/01_Core_Foundations/03_SMT_SIMD_and_Vector_Execution.md) |
| **VHDL** | A strongly typed hardware description language, standardized as IEEE 1076. It coexists with SystemVerilog and is common in aerospace, defense, and European industry. | — |
| **Via** | A vertical connection between adjacent metal layers. Vias dominate interconnect resistance at advanced nodes and are a leading defect site, which is why doubling and pillars exist. | [Routing and Parasitic Extraction](05_Backend_Physical_Design/06_Routing_and_Parasitic_Extraction.md) |
| **Via pillar** | A stacked column of vias and short metal segments used to connect a cell pin up to a higher, lower-resistance layer. It reduces via and thin-metal resistance on critical nets at the cost of blocking routing tracks. | [Routing and Parasitic Extraction](05_Backend_Physical_Design/06_Routing_and_Parasitic_Extraction.md) |
| **VIP (verification IP)** | A pre-built, protocol-compliant verification component for a standard interface such as AXI or PCIe. It exists because writing a compliant protocol checker is a multi-month job that nobody should repeat. | [UVM Methodology](03_Frontend_RTL_and_Verification/10_UVM_Methodology.md) |
| **VIPT (virtually indexed, physically tagged)** | A cache indexed with virtual address bits while the tag comparison uses the physical address. It overlaps TLB lookup with array access, and it constrains cache size to way size not exceeding page size unless aliasing is handled. | [Cache Microarchitecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/04_Cache_Hierarchy/01_Cache_Microarchitecture.md) |
| **Voltage area** | The physical region a power domain occupies in the floorplan. It exists because a supply must be routed as a contiguous grid, so a logical domain has to become a place. | [Low-Power Architecture and Domain Partitioning](02_Power_and_Low_Power/03_Low_Power_Architecture_and_Domain_Partitioning.md) |
| **Voltage droop** | A transient supply dip when current demand rises faster than the delivery network can respond, governed by $L\,di/dt$ and grid impedance. | **vplan (verification plan)** | The structured mapping from specification features to coverage items and tests. | **Vt (threshold voltage)** | The gate voltage at which the channel begins to conduct strongly. | **VTC (voltage transfer characteristic)** | The DC plot of a gate's output against its input. Its slope and unity-gain points define noise margins, which is where the digital abstraction is quantitatively justified. | [CMOS Fundamentals](00_Fundamentals/01_CMOS_Fundamentals.md) |

## W

| Term / acronym | Definition | Where it is derived |
|---|---|---|
| **Wavefront allocator** | An allocator that sweeps priority diagonally across a request matrix, producing a maximal match with good fairness. Its long combinational chain is what usually keeps it out of high-frequency routers. | [Router Microarchitecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/04_On_Chip_Networks/03_Router_Microarchitecture.md) |
| **W1C (write-one-to-clear)** | A register field cleared by writing a 1 to it. It exists so that a driver can clear the specific status bits it has handled without a read-modify-write race against bits hardware set in the meantime. | [IP Reuse, Integration and Register Automation](08_Cross_Cutting_Engineering/04_IP_Reuse_Integration_and_Register_Automation.md) |
| **Wafer** | The silicon disc on which many dies are fabricated together. All cost accounting starts from cost per wafer divided by good dies per wafer, which is why area and yield dominate economics. | [Fabrication Process](07_Manufacturing_and_Bringup/01_Fabrication_Process.md) |
| **Wafer sort** | Testing dies while still on the wafer, before dicing and packaging. It exists so that defective dies are never packaged, since packaging cost exceeds the value of the test that avoids it. | [Tapeout and Post-Silicon Bring-up](07_Manufacturing_and_Bringup/03_Tapeout_and_Post_Silicon_Bringup.md) |
| **Wakeup (scheduler)** | Broadcasting a result tag so dependent instructions in the issue queue mark their operand ready. The wakeup-select loop must close in a single cycle for back-to-back dependent issue, which is why it constrains issue queue size directly. | [Advanced Scheduling, Wakeup and Replay](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/03_Out_of_Order_Backend/04_Advanced_Scheduling_Wakeup_and_Replay.md) |
| **Wallace / Dadda tree** | Partial-product reduction networks built from carry-save adders, reaching logarithmic depth. They differ in how aggressively they compress at each level, trading adder count against wiring regularity. | [Adders and Multipliers](00_Fundamentals/03_Adders_and_Multipliers.md) |
| **Warp** | A group of GPU threads (32 on NVIDIA hardware) executing in lockstep under one instruction. It is the unit of scheduling, of divergence, and of memory coalescing. | [SIMT Scheduling and Occupancy](01_Architecture_and_PPA/02_GPU_Architecture/01_Core_Architecture/02_SIMT_Scheduling_and_Occupancy.md) |
| **Warpage** | Out-of-plane deformation of die or package from thermal expansion mismatch. It causes bump non-contact and cracking, and it constrains how large and how thin a package can be. | [IC Packaging](07_Manufacturing_and_Bringup/02_IC_Packaging.md) |
| **Watchdog** | A timer that resets or alerts if software fails to service it. It is the last-resort safety mechanism against a hung system, and its coverage argument depends entirely on where the service points are placed. | [Functional Safety and Reliability Engineering](08_Cross_Cutting_Engineering/02_Functional_Safety_and_Reliability_Engineering.md) |
| **Well / well tap** | The doped region hosting transistors of the opposite type, and the contact that ties it to a supply. Without regular taps the well potential floats and latch-up becomes possible. | [CMOS Fundamentals](00_Fundamentals/01_CMOS_Fundamentals.md) |
| **Wire bond** | Connecting die pads to package leads with fine wire. It is cheap and limited to the die perimeter with relatively high inductance, which is why high-performance parts moved to flip chip. | [IC Packaging](07_Manufacturing_and_Bringup/02_IC_Packaging.md) |
| **Wireload model (WLM)** | A statistical estimate of net capacitance from fanout, used before placement exists. It is obsolete at advanced nodes because the estimate error exceeds the timing margin, which is the reason physical synthesis replaced it. | [Physical Synthesis and Design Planning](04_Synthesis/05_Physical_Synthesis_and_Design_Planning.md) |
| **WLP (wafer-level packaging)** | Packaging performed on the whole wafer before dicing, producing a package barely larger than the die. It is the cheapest and smallest option and is limited in IO count, which is what fan-out WLP extends. | [IC Packaging](07_Manufacturing_and_Bringup/02_IC_Packaging.md) |
| **WNS (worst negative slack)** | The most negative slack in the design. It sets the maximum frequency, so it is the headline number of every timing report. | [Synthesis Flow and QoR Closure](04_Synthesis/04_Synthesis_Flow_and_QoR_Closure.md) |
| **Wormhole routing** | Forwarding a packet's flits through a router as soon as the head flit has claimed an output, without waiting for the whole packet. It cuts latency and buffer requirements and creates the possibility of a packet spanning several routers, which is where deadlock comes from. | [Routing, Flow Control and Deadlock](01_Architecture_and_PPA/04_SoC_and_Chiplet_Architecture/04_On_Chip_Networks/02_Routing_Flow_Control_and_Deadlock.md) |
| **Writeback / write-through** | Cache write policies: hold the dirty line until eviction, or forward every write to the next level. Writeback saves bandwidth and requires dirty tracking and coherence writeback handling; write-through simplifies both and costs traffic. | [Cache Microarchitecture](01_Architecture_and_PPA/01_CPU_Architecture/04_Cache_Hierarchy/01_Cache_Microarchitecture.md) |

| **X (unknown)** | The four-state simulation value meaning "not known".
